The Loop · How an incident actually flows

Good troubleshooting is a loop you run under pressure: assess before you touch anything, orient on what changed, isolate the cause by elimination, resolve (or fall back), then feed what you learned back in. Panic skips straight to "resolve" — and usually makes it worse.

The Escalation Ladder · Where to look, in order

When you don't know where the problem lives, walk the ladder from your side of the fence outward. Most incidents resolve on the first two rungs — the discipline is asking the right questions before blaming the layer below you.

Plan B · When you're stuck, stop guessing

Random fixes under pressure turn one incident into two. When you're out of leads, switch from finding the cause to stopping the bleeding.

Admitting you need more time or help is good engineering. The goal is a healthy system, not a hero. Buy time with a fallback, then debug calmly.
